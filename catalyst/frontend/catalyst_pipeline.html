Catalyst requirement pipeline

Pick a diene and dienophile. The pipeline builds their Hückel matrix, runs a low-precision or analog front end, refines the result exactly, and reports what an ideal catalyst environment would need at each atom.

    Rank is within each fragment by the asynchronicity lever. “Sign stable” is the share of parameter variants (hO, bond set, γ) that give the same requirement.

    Model limits: simple Hückel theory has no σ framework, sterics, solvent, geometry or Pauli repulsion. The requirements are a labelled heuristic, checked against known chemistry in three ways: Lewis-acid and H-bond catalysis at the carbonyl oxygen comes out as “withdrawing”, the all-carbon pair gives no lever, and an ether oxygen on the diene comes out as “donating”.

    The analog front end is a behavioral simulation, not hardware. In this design it cannot save energy: the exact refinement alone costs about as much as diagonalizing H directly.
