    What imperfect arithmetic costs you

    The same pipeline on deliberately degraded arithmetic. At eight atoms the answer stays right — what you pay is extra exact refinement steps afterwards. Grow the system and the tolerance collapses: past about 24 atoms, noise that was free at eight atoms stops the pipeline converging at all.

    What this does not claim

    Simple Hückel theory has no σ framework, sterics, solvent, geometry or Pauli repulsion, so a catalyst that is electronically ideal and physically in the way looks ideal here. The analog array is a behavioural simulation, not hardware. This design cannot save energy: the exact refinement alone costs about as much as diagonalising the matrix directly. And the asynchronicity lever became the primary observable only after the interaction-energy lever failed a control — that choice was made after seeing results.

    What to actually put there

    The rule table says which atom and which direction. Turning that into a catalyst needs one more filter from the model, and then chemistry the model does not contain. Both halves are below, kept apart on purpose.

    Step 1 — which levers can anything actually reach

    Seven of the eight atoms are carbons inside the π system. A catalyst can only influence those through space, by sitting nearby as a charge or a field. One atom, the carbonyl oxygen, has a lone pair that a group can be aimed at. The pipeline labels this itself, and it changes the answer completely.

    This does not rescue the failed prediction. The pre-registered guess was that the carbonyl oxygen would rank in the top two by magnitude, and it does not — it is fourth, in all 18 settings tested. The filter above is a separate observation: among atoms a chemist can actually put something on, it ranks first. Both statements are true and the first one is still a miss.

    Step 2 — how hard should it pull

    Dragging hO makes the carbonyl oxygen more electron-withdrawing, which is how a coordinated Lewis acid or a hydrogen-bond donor is represented in a Hückel model. Everything below is recomputed live.

    Step 3 — classes of catalyst that do this

    These are not model output. The model produced a requirement; this is the published chemistry that implements it, listed so the requirement can be checked against something real. Nothing here is ranked by the model — it cannot rank them, for the reasons in step 4.

    Lewis acids on the carbonyl oxygen

    direct match

    A metal centre coordinates the carbonyl lone pair and pulls electron density out of the dienophile. This is the closest real-world analogue of what the model does to the oxygen, and the oldest trick in the field.

    AlCl3 · BF3 · TiCl4 · SnCl4 · ZnCl2 · Li+

    A DFT study ordered their catalytic strength as I2 < SnCl4 < TiCl4 < ZnCl2 < BF3 < AlCl3. The model cannot reproduce that ordering — it has no metal, no geometry and no sterics.

    Hydrogen-bond donors

    direct match

    Two N–H bonds clamp the carbonyl oxygen and withdraw density without any metal. This is exactly the requirement the model prints for that atom: “withdrawing — H-bond donor or Lewis acid on the lone pair”.

    Schreiner's thiourea, N,N′-bis[3,5-bis(trifluoromethyl)phenyl]thiourea · squaramides · chiral diols (TADDOL-type)

    Attractive for a pitch because it is metal-free and works in water. The model says nothing about which donor, or about enantioselectivity.

    Iminium organocatalysis

    out of model scope

    Acrolein is an α,β-unsaturated aldehyde — precisely the substrate class MacMillan's imidazolidinone catalysts were built for. A chiral secondary amine condenses with the aldehyde to form an iminium ion, lowering the LUMO the way a Lewis acid would.

    First- and second-generation imidazolidinones (Ahrendt, Borths & MacMillan, JACS 2000)

    The model cannot score this one. Iminium formation is covalent: it replaces the C=O with a C=N+, which is a different molecule, not a perturbed one. Our δ at the oxygen cannot represent it. Worth naming in a pitch as the obvious real-world route, but it is outside what was computed.

    Proteins

    partial match

    A designed Diels–Alderase, DA_20_00 (PDB 3I1C), was built on the same reasoning: a hydrogen-bond donor aimed at the dienophile carbonyl to lower its LUMO, and an acceptor at the diene to raise its HOMO. Among natural enzymes, SpnF places a threonine hydrogen bond on the substrate carbonyl; AbyU instead works by forcing the substrate into shape.

    DA_20_00 / PDB 3I1C · SpnF · AbyU / PDB 5DYV

    The designed enzyme agrees with the model on the oxygen and on direction. AbyU is a warning: it catalyses mainly through geometry, which this model has none of.

    Step 4 — what the model cannot tell you

    • It cannot pick a catalyst. With no sterics, geometry or solvent, AlCl3 and BF3 are the same object to this model. It narrows the search to a site and a direction; a chemist picks the reagent.
    • It cannot do enantioselectivity, which is what most of the modern literature is actually for. The model has no three-dimensional structure, so it has no face to select.
    • It cannot represent covalent catalysis, which rules out the iminium route above.
    • Its textbook justification is contested. Recent DFT work argues the frontier-orbital story for Lewis acid catalysis is wrong, and that relief of Pauli repulsion is what lowers the barrier.

    The one place that last point cuts the other way. The same work reports that Lewis acids make the reaction markedly more asynchronous — in their calculations the difference between the two forming bonds grows from about 0.46 Å uncatalysed to 0.78 Å with AlCl3 and 0.91 Å with Li+, and protonation tips it fully stepwise. Asynchronicity is the quantity this pipeline tracks, and the scan in step 2 moves the same way for the same reason. The two are not numerically comparable — ours is a bond-order difference in units of |β|, theirs a bond length in ångström — but the observable that was picked here post-hoc turns out to be the one that recent mechanistic work says actually changes. That is a point in favour of the observable, not of the Hückel justification that was originally given for it.

    07Report

    Plain-text record of this exact run, ready to paste into notes.

    Model limits. Simple Hückel theory has no σ framework, sterics, solvent, geometry or Pauli repulsion. The requirements are a labelled heuristic, checked against known chemistry in three ways: Lewis-acid and H-bond catalysis at the carbonyl oxygen comes out as “withdrawing”, the all-carbon pair gives no lever, and an ether oxygen on the diene comes out as “donating”.

    Hardware. The analog front end is a behavioural simulation, not hardware; offset and noise levels are placeholders. In this design it cannot save energy: the exact refinement alone costs about as much as diagonalising H directly.

    Disclosure. The asynchronicity lever was made primary after the interaction-energy lever failed the carbonyl-oxygen control — a post-hoc choice. The pre-registered prediction that the carbonyl oxygen would rank in the top two by magnitude failed in 18 of 18 settings.
